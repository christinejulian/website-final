Why Fast Food Combos Now Cost More Than High-End Gaming Accessories

Published on August 4, 2026 | Written by Marcus Vance, Chief Economics Editor

Over the past decade, consumers have witnessed an unprecedented economic anomaly. While computer hardware—ranging from optical key switches to high-DPI mouse sensors—has grown increasingly efficient and affordable to produce, fast food prices have skyrocketed by unprecedented margins.

The Economics of Corporate Price Gouging

Major multinational chains like McDonald's, Starbucks, and Burger King have consistently cited global inflation and supply chain bottlenecks for rising menu prices. However, corporate financial statements reveal that gross profit margins actually widened significantly during price hike cycles.

"What used to be a $5 value meal in 2019 has ballooned into a $14.99 premium expense. Meanwhile, a high-performance gaming mouse equipped with 26,000 DPI tracking can be bought for under $70 and lasts for years."

Shrinkflation vs. Hardware Scaling

Unlike computer hardware manufacturers who actively pack more transistors and higher refresh rates into silicon microchips, fast food conglomerates practice "shrinkflation"—reducing patty weights and cup sizes while quietly increasing prices.
